guide: How Book Dude works For grown-ups How Book Dude works The short version She reads, takes quizzes, and earns points. Every 10 points, she picks one: $10 in her Greenlight account or 60 minutes of screen time. Most of it runs by itself. Grown-ups mostly approve things. Where points come from AR quizzes at school. When the school emails a quiz result, it lands in Book Dude by itself, with its points. Nothing to type. Library tests in the COBOL tab. Articles with a short test. Passing one earns 2 points for a short article or 4 for a long one, plus 1 bonus point for answering the think-about-it question. Home points. For a book the school has no AR quiz for, a grown-up can give points on that book’s page (Home points box, passcode needed). Spelling BB (Games tab). Spelling a word right twice in a row makes it a gem. Every 10 gems earn 1 point on One Bee, 2 on Two Bee, 3 on Three Bee; finishing a list adds 5, 10 or 15; Bee-round streaks of 10, 25 and 50 add 1 each. A word she had missed before counts double. Each word pays once, and points are never taken back. All of these count the same toward money and screen time. AR points are also tracked on their own on the Progress tab, against the school goal: 62.5 a quarter, 250 for the year. Library tests, home points and spelling never count as AR. Spending 10 points On the Progress tab, Fig’s litter box gets cleaner with every point. At 10 it’s clean, and two buttons appear: $10 in her Greenlight account or 60 minutes. It’s her choice; no passcode. Money: everyone on the Sunday email list (below) gets an email saying to add the money. Add it, then tap Grown-up: mark as deposited on the Book Money Jar. Minutes: they wait on the Screen time card until she cashes them in (below). Tapped the wrong one? Settings → Book money and screen time → Her choices fixes it. Screen time and piano One bank of minutes, for any screen: iPad, Switch, laptop or TV. Each device keeps its own normal daily limit; these are extra. Finishing a book from the Book Dude list adds 30 extra minutes (a few special books earn more). Cashing in opens Friday after school (3 pm) through Sunday, and only once she has practiced piano 90 minutes that week (Monday to Sunday). She taps Cash in, a grown-up enters the passcode and picks how many minutes she’s using (15, 30, 60 or all). Then give her the time on that device, or start a timer for the TV. Sick week or a holiday? Grown-up: cash in anyway skips the rules. Piano: on the Piano practice card, tap + for each 15 minutes she practices (passcode). Piano doesn’t earn minutes; it unlocks cashing in. The Sunday email Every Sunday morning the grown-ups on the list get an email about her week: quizzes and points, money and minutes waiting, books finished and being read, Library tests passed, a few dinner-table questions about each book, and a summary of her chats with COBOL (anything worrying is emailed right away, too). The same list gets the money emails. The list is kept in the school-email Google script, not in Book Dude, so a Book Dude invite or sign-in doesn’t add anyone. The grown-up who set up the script adds each address there (Script Properties, DIGEST_TO, with commas between addresses). The grown-up passcode A 4-digit passcode keeps the grown-up parts grown-up: entering points, approving screen time, checking off piano, marking money deposited, and changing settings. Once typed, it stays open for 5 minutes. Forgot it? Forgot the passcode? on the passcode box resets it. Your to-do list During the week: tap + on Piano practice after she practices. Friday to Sunday: approve screen time when she cashes it in, and give it on the device. Sunday: read the weekly email, and ask her a dinner-table question or two. When the money email comes: add it to her Greenlight account, then mark it deposited. Now and then: give home points for a book with no AR quiz, and once a quarter, check the AR card against the school’s report (Grown-up: match the school’s AR report).
